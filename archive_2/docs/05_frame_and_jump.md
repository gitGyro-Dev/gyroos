# Frame and Jump

This document defines Frame, Frame transition, and Jump in Gyro Logic.
These concepts explain how stable structures change, how new identities emerge, and how systems undergo structural transformation.

---

## 1. Frame

### 1.1 Concept

A Frame is a structural context in which certain patterns are considered stable and meaningful.

A Frame defines:

- what structures are observable
- what transformations are allowed
- what is considered stable

Different Frames yield different interpretations of the same underlying Field.

---

### 1.2 Frame as Stability Context

Within a given Frame, certain structures appear stable and recognizable.

Formally, a Frame defines:

- the Slice operator used
- the stability criteria
- the conservation rules

Thus, stability and identity are always Frame-dependent.

---

## 2. Frame Transition

### 2.1 Motivation

As a system evolves, the current Frame may become insufficient to maintain stability.

This manifests as:

- increasing instability
- loss of convergence
- failure of identity preservation

When instability exceeds a threshold, a Frame transition occurs.

---

### 2.2 Jump

A Jump is a transition from one Frame to another.

    Frame A → Frame B

A Jump corresponds to:

- breakdown of previous stability
- expansion of Void
- emergence of new stable structures

Jumps are typically irreversible.

---

### 2.3 Properties of Jump

A Jump has the following properties:

- It cannot be fully controlled.
- It can be anticipated through instability patterns.
- It is generally irreversible.
- After a Jump, previous structures are reinterpreted within the new Frame.

This means that past identities may no longer hold after a Jump.

---

## 3. Role of Instability

Instability plays a constructive role in Gyro Logic.

Instability indicates:

- misalignment between structure and Frame
- insufficiency of current Slice
- potential for structural reorganization

Thus, instability is not failure but a precursor to transformation.

---

## 4. Void and Frame Transition

Void represents regions where stable structures cannot form under the current Frame.

During a Frame transition:

- Void expands
- existing structures lose stability
- new structures emerge from Void

Void is therefore the medium through which new Frames arise.

---

## 5. Identity Across Frames

Identity is not absolute across Frame transitions.

When a Frame changes:

- previously stable identities may dissolve
- new identities may emerge
- some structures may persist if they are robust enough

This explains both continuity and transformation of identity.

---

## 6. Frame Hierarchy

Frames can be organized hierarchically.

Higher-level Frames may:

- integrate multiple lower-level Frames
- resolve conflicts between them
- provide new forms of stability

This leads to nested structures of meaning and identity.

---

## Summary

- Frames define the conditions for stability and meaning.
- Instability leads to Frame transitions (Jumps).
- Jumps are irreversible structural transformations.
- Void plays a central role in enabling new structures.
- Identity may be preserved, transformed, or lost across Frame transitions.
